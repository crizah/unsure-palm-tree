Free Food in Bangalore: Indira Canteen, NGOs & More (2025 Guide)

Bangalore has a well-established network of free and subsidised food resources, from BBMP's Indira Canteens to NGO kitchens and religious community meals. This guide covers everything you need to know to find a free meal near you in the city.

Indira Canteen Bangalore

The Indira Canteen scheme is Bangalore's government-run subsidised meal programme, operated by the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP). Launched in 2017, it now operates at over 190 locations across the city and serves hundreds of thousands of meals every day.

Meals are priced to be accessible to everyone:

  • Breakfast: ₹5 (idli, vada, or upma)
  • Lunch: ₹10 (rice, sambar, rasam, vegetable, and papad)
  • Dinner: ₹10 (same as lunch)

Operating hours are generally 7:30 am to 11 am for breakfast, 12 pm to 3 pm for lunch, and 7 pm to 9 pm for dinner, though this varies by location. The BBMP manages the scheme; for official information visit bbmp.gov.in.

NGOs Serving Free Food in Bangalore

Alongside the government canteen scheme, a number of large NGOs operate active free food programmes across Bangalore.

Akshaya Patra Foundation

The Akshaya Patra Foundation is headquartered in Bangalore and is one of the world's largest NGO-run school meal programmes. Beyond school mid-day meals, it runs community kitchen programmes serving free cooked food to underprivileged communities. Its central kitchen in Bangalore has a capacity of over 100,000 meals per day.

Feeding India, Bangalore Chapter

Feeding India, now under the Zomato Foundation, has an active Bangalore chapter that runs food rescue and distribution operations. Volunteers collect surplus food from hotels, restaurants, and corporate cafeterias and redistribute it to communities in areas like KR Market, Shivajinagar, and near labour gathering points on the outskirts of the city. More at feedingindia.org.

Robin Hood Army Bangalore

Robin Hood Army operates every weekend in Bangalore, collecting surplus food from restaurants and distributing it to people sleeping rough or gathering at public spaces. The Bangalore chapter is one of the most active in India, with regular distribution in areas like Majestic, City Railway Station, and Cubbon Park.

Khana Chahiye

Khana Chahiye is a hyperlocal initiative that links people who have surplus cooked food with people who need it in Bangalore. They operate through a network of community volunteers and have served hundreds of thousands of meals since starting operations.

Religious Community Kitchens in Bangalore

Religious institutions in Bangalore run some of the most reliable and accessible free food programmes in the city.

Gurudwara Sri Guru Singh Sabha

The main Gurudwara in Bangalore (located in Frazer Town) runs a daily langar that is open to everyone. Like all Gurudwara langars, entry is free, no registration is required, and food is served regardless of religion, caste, or background.

Iskcon Temple Bangalore

The Iskcon Temple on Hare Krishna Hill, Rajajinagar, runs a prasad distribution and has a large community hall where devotees and visitors receive meals. They also conduct periodic free distribution events and operate Govinda's restaurant for affordable sattvic meals.

Dharmasthala Community Kitchen

The Dharmasthala Dharmadhikari trust runs community feeding programmes in and around Bangalore, particularly serving pilgrims and underprivileged families. Free meals are available to all visitors on designated days.

Cheap Food for Daily Workers in Bangalore

If you are a construction worker, autorickshaw driver, domestic worker, or daily wage earner in Bangalore, here is a practical guide:

  • Nearest Indira Canteen: the best option for a hot meal at ₹10. Use GeoHowl's map to find the one closest to your worksite or home.
  • Gurudwara langar: completely free, no questions asked. The Sri Guru Singh Sabha in Frazer Town is accessible from most parts of the city.
  • Akshaya Patra community distribution: check their current schedule for distribution near your area.
  • Robin Hood Army: weekend distributions near Majestic and City Railway Station are reliable.

Research from the World Food Programme consistently identifies casual urban labourers and migrants as among the most food-insecure groups in Indian cities, which is exactly why programmes like Indira Canteen exist and why mapping them matters.
