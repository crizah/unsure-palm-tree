Free Food in Delhi

Delhi has one of the largest networks of subsidised food in India. The Atal Canteen scheme (also called Indra Café) operates across all 272 wards, serving breakfast for ₹5 and full meals for ₹10. Beyond government canteens, dozens of NGOs run daily free food programmes for the poor, the elderly, and daily wage workers.

Major Gurudwaras in Delhi — including Bangla Sahib and Sis Ganj Sahib — run 24-hour langars that are open to anyone regardless of religion or background. Many distribute thousands of meals a day at no cost.

Free Food in Bangalore

Bangalore's Indira Canteen network, run by BBMP, serves subsidised meals at ₹5 for breakfast and ₹10 for lunch and dinner at over 190 locations across the city. For daily wage workers and migrant labourers especially, these canteens make a real difference.

Several large NGOs are also active in Bangalore — including the Akshaya Patra Foundation, which serves mid-day meals, and the Robin Hood Army, which distributes surplus food from restaurants to those who need it.

Who Is This For?

  • Daily wage workers and construction labourers looking for cheap food near their worksite
  • Students on a tight budget who need an affordable meal
  • Elderly people without family support in the city
  • Migrants who have recently arrived and do not know where to look
  • Anyone facing temporary food insecurity — no registration or ID required at most points
