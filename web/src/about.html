About Crizah — Free Food Locator

We build tools to help people find free food and cheap meals in their city. No sign-up. No ads. Just a map of every canteen, NGO kitchen and community feeding point near you.

Our Mission

Millions of people in Indian cities live with daily food insecurity — not because food does not exist nearby, but because they do not know where to find it. Government canteens, NGO kitchens, religious langars, and community distribution points collectively serve millions of free and subsidised meals every day. The problem is discoverability.

Crizah exists to close that gap. We map every free and cheap food resource we can verify so that anyone — a daily wage worker, a student, an elderly person — can find a meal near them in under a minute.

What We Map

Our database currently covers Delhi and Bangalore and includes:

  • Government canteens — Atal Canteens (Delhi) and Indira Canteens (Bangalore) run by the state government and BBMP respectively, serving meals at ₹5–10
  • NGO kitchens — feeding programmes run by organisations like Feeding India, Roti Bank, Robin Hood Army, and local charities
  • Religious langars and annadanams — Gurudwaras, temples, and mosques that serve free food to all visitors daily
  • Mobile distribution points — locations where food is distributed on a fixed schedule by civic volunteers or NGOs

Each entry includes the name, address, type, and known operating hours. We do not list a place unless we can verify it is currently active.

Cities We Cover

We currently cover Delhi and Bangalore. These two cities have the largest documented networks of free food resources in India, making them the best starting point.

Use the interactive map at geo.crizah.space/locate to explore locations by area or find the nearest point to your current location.

How to Find Free Food Near You

  1. Go to the locator map.
  2. Tap Near me to find places close to your current location, or type a neighbourhood in the search box.
  3. Use the city and type filters to narrow results.
  4. Tap any pin to see the full address, type, and timings.
  5. Always verify timings before visiting — hours can change on holidays or during special events.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register or carry ID?

At government canteens and most NGO kitchens, no registration or ID is required. Religious langars are open to everyone without any requirement. Some NGO programmes may ask you to sign in; check the individual listing.

Is the food really free?

Government canteens charge a nominal amount (₹5–10) to cover a small portion of costs — this counts as subsidised, not strictly free. NGO and religious sites are typically fully free. We label each type clearly on the map.

How do you keep the data up to date?

We verify listings regularly and remove places that have closed. If you find an error or want to suggest a location, the information is maintained in an open database.

What is cheap food for workers near me?

If you are a construction worker, domestic worker, or daily wage earner, government canteens are your best bet — a full meal for ₹10 is available at Atal Canteens in Delhi and Indira Canteens in Bangalore. Many are open from early morning through evening. Open the map to find the one nearest your worksite.
