Free Food in Delhi: Atal Canteens, NGOs & Distribution Points (2025 Guide)

Delhi has one of the largest networks of free and subsidised food in India. This guide covers every major source — government canteens, NGOs, religious langars, and distribution points — so you can find a meal wherever you are in the city.

Atal Canteens in Delhi (Indra Café)

The Atal Canteen scheme — also marketed as Indra Café — is Delhi's flagship subsidised food programme run by the Government of Delhi. As of 2025, canteens operate in all 272 wards across the city.

Meal prices are kept deliberately low to make them accessible to daily wage workers, domestic helpers, construction labourers, and anyone else struggling with food costs:

  • Breakfast — ₹5 (usually poha, upma, or puri sabzi)
  • Lunch — ₹10 (dal, sabzi, rice, and two rotis)
  • Dinner — ₹10 (similar to lunch menu)

Canteens typically open from 7 am to 10 am for breakfast and 12 pm to 3 pm and 7 pm to 9 pm for meals, though timings vary by location. For the official list of canteen locations, visit the Atal Canteen Delhi website.

NGOs Serving Free Meals in Delhi

Beyond government schemes, a large number of NGOs in Delhi run daily free food programmes targeting the most vulnerable — the homeless, the elderly, and those without income.

Feeding India (Zomato Foundation)

Feeding India, now operating under the Zomato Foundation, runs one of the largest food rescue and distribution networks in the country. In Delhi, volunteers collect surplus food from restaurants, weddings, and events and redistribute it to communities in need. They also operate fixed distribution points. Learn more at feedingindia.org.

Roti Bank Delhi

Roti Bank operates a network of food collection and distribution across Delhi. Donors can contribute cooked meals or dry rations, which volunteers distribute to homeless individuals and migrant labourers. They maintain distribution points near major bus terminals and labour gathering points.

Deepalaya

Deepalaya is a Delhi-based NGO that has worked with marginalised communities since 1979. Among its programmes is regular community feeding for children and families in Delhi's resettlement colonies and slum areas.

Robin Hood Army Delhi

Robin Hood Army is a volunteer-run organisation that collects surplus food from restaurants every weekend and distributes it to those sleeping rough or gathering at public spaces. Delhi has one of their most active chapters.

Religious Langars in Delhi

Gurudwara langars are arguably the most important source of truly free food in Delhi. The langar tradition in Sikhism means every Gurudwara is obligated to feed anyone who comes, regardless of religion, caste, or background.

Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

One of the most prominent Gurudwaras in Delhi, Bangla Sahib serves langar 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Located in Connaught Place, it feeds tens of thousands of people daily. No registration, no ID, and no payment is ever required.

Gurudwara Sis Ganj Sahib

Located in Chandni Chowk, Sis Ganj Sahib also runs a continuous langar. The kitchens operate around the clock and serve simple, nutritious meals.

Birla Mandir and Other Temples

Several temples in Delhi run annadanam (food donation) programmes, particularly on festival days and weekends. The Iskcon Temple in East of Kailash runs a large Govinda's prasad distribution regularly.

Free Food for Daily Workers in Delhi

If you are a construction worker, domestic worker, or daily wage earner in Delhi, here is a practical summary:

  • Nearest Atal Canteen — a full meal for ₹10, available in every ward. Use Crizah's map to find the one closest to your worksite.
  • Gurudwara langar — completely free, no questions asked. Major Gurudwaras are spread across all parts of Delhi.
  • NGO distribution — Feeding India and Roti Bank have fixed points near labour-dense areas like Sadar Bazaar, Azadpur Mandi, and near ISBT Kashmere Gate.

According to the World Food Programme, urban food insecurity in India disproportionately affects casual labourers and migrants — exactly the people these programmes are designed to reach.
